The moment the intro of Brother Xiaoqi's song rings out,


Picking up memories, youth all comes back,


And the heart is healed just like that,


This is a scene I can never tire of,


This is a blessing.


Once you speak, it's the jianghu,


Who didn't dream of the jianghu in youth,


Half a lifetime weathered like a beast of burden,


Not letting down the best years, drunk on the dust of the world,


Fearless of tomorrow's stormy road.


Youth is quite abstract,


In youth, a psycho,


In middle age, he looks like a madman instead,


In our teens, we were as bland as plain water—every day pretty much the same,


In your twenties, you say we have no money now, how about buying a box of instant noodles to get through the month,


In your thirties, one day you say when I turn 38 I can treat you to a glass of Moutai,


Murmuring,


Two old boys racing toward forty, heading out to hear a sixty-something sing,


That night,


A belated encounter—how much youth do you pick up,


Off-key,


Those years, a small southern coastal town, an old triangular cassette player playing music,


Little one,


Under the swaying trees of high summer, us 2Bs, with only a patch of ground to ourselves,


Folded in half,


There's a kind of warmth called "I'm still here",


You see,


Willn't the world one has to look up to be so hard to reach?
